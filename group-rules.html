Giving and Receiving Story Feedback

Our writing group is a supportive community of writers helping writers. The purpose of feedback is not to edit, judge, or rewrite someone else's work, but to help the writer better understand how their writing is experienced by readers.

Before We Begin

When Receiving Feedback

  • Listen without interrupting, defending, or explaining the work.
  • Remember that feedback reflects a reader's experience of the piece.
  • Consider recurring comments carefully.
  • You are not required to use every suggestion.
  • Thank readers for their time and attention.

Respectful Communication

  • Offer feedback you would be comfortable sharing openly with the group.
  • Be honest, but always be kind and constructive.

Keep Feedback Within the Group

We recognize that writing is something many of our members are passionate about. That passion often leads to thoughtful discussions, strong opinions, and a genuine desire to help fellow writers improve. We encourage that enthusiasm while also respecting each writer's personal boundaries.

  • Keep feedback within scheduled group meeting times. Feedback and discussion about another member's writing should take place during scheduled group meetings unless the writer has explicitly invited further discussion outside of those times.
  • Respect personal boundaries. Do not send unsolicited feedback through email, text, social media, or other private channels. Having someone's contact information does not imply permission to provide additional critiques or commentary. Both parties must agree in advance to continue discussing a piece of writing privately.
  • Be mindful of what you share. Feedback should always be respectful, constructive, and something you would feel comfortable expressing openly in front of the group.

Our intention is not to limit creative discussion, but to ensure that every writer feels comfortable sharing their work without feeling obligated to engage in conversations about it outside of scheduled group meeting times, unless they have explicitly agreed to do so.

When Sharing Your Work

  • Let the writing speak for itself.
  • Provide only the title of your piece and begin reading.
  • Do not explain, justify, or provide background information before or after reading.
  • When finished, a simple “thank you” is enough.
  • Provide a content warning if your piece contains potentially distressing or sensitive subject matter.

When Giving Feedback

Focus on the writing, not on editing. Grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting are concerns for later drafts unless they significantly affect meaning or understanding.

1. What did I like?

Respond as a reader. What moved you, interested you, surprised you, or stayed with you?

2. What stood out?

Focus on the craft of the writing, including imagery, dialogue, voice, structure, description, word choice, point of view, and detail.

3. What questions do I have?

Frame suggestions as questions whenever possible. Writers do not need to answer these questions during the feedback session.

Be Specific

General comments are less useful than specific observations. Identify the scene, image, line, or moment that created your response.

Our Commitment

We are not editors or critics. We are writers supporting writers. We listen carefully, respond thoughtfully, and provide feedback that is specific, respectful, honest, and encouraging.
